AI Policy for SP23 English Courses
The following policy was developed specifically for my SP23 English courses. 
It takes into account what I intend to do with AI (ChatGPT and Midjourney) in my classes.

This is not a mandatory policy for anyone else’s syllabi, but I do suggest that everyone 
include some language in their own that acknowledges these tools and clarifies how you 
intend to use them (or not use them):

AI Policy – Artificial Intelligence (AI)
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is one of the emerging Web3 tools that promises to shake up 
both the academic and professional landscape. Regardless of how you feel about it, learning 
to use it well is an emerging and valuable skill.

That being said, you can expect to use AI in my classes (ChatGPT and Midjourney AI, at a minimum), 
and I will provide tutorials in Canvas about how to use them. I’m also happy to help with these 
tools during office hours.

Please be aware:

If you provide minimum prompts, you will get low-quality results.
You will need to refine your prompts in order to achieve better outcomes. This will take work.

Don’t trust anything AI (ChatGPT) says.
Assume facts it gives you are wrong unless you already know the answer or can check it with 
another source. You will be responsible for any errors or omissions provided by the tool. 
It works best for topics you understand.

Any use of AI tools must be acknowledged.
Please be sure to include a paragraph at the end of any assignment that uses AI explaining:

What you used it for

The specific prompts you used to get your results

Failure to do so is a violation of academic honesty policies.

— adapted from the work of Ethan Mollick (Wharton School)
