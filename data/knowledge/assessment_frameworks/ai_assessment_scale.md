# AI Assessment Scale (AIAS)

## Source
Furze, L., Perkins, M., Roe, J., & MacVaugh, J. (2024, August 28). Updating the AI Assessment Scale. Leon Furze. https://leonfurze.com/2024/08/28/updating-the-ai-assessment-scale/

## Overview
The AI Assessment Scale (AIAS) is a framework developed to help educators determine appropriate levels of AI use in assessments. It has been adopted by schools and universities worldwide and has been promoted by the Australian Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) as a tool for assessment transparency.

## Background
The AIAS was developed in response to the need for more than a binary "use or don't use" solution for AI and assessment. The framework addresses both acute problems (immediate threats to academic integrity) and chronic problems (fundamental questions about assessment and learning in the AI age).

The scale has been refined through:
- Feedback from diverse perspectives (teachers, students, lecturers, disability activists)
- Input from experts in assessment security, cognitive sciences, and pedagogy
- Adaptations and translations worldwide
- Consideration of technological advances in generative AI

## The AI Assessment Scale (Updated August 2024)

The updated AIAS provides a 5-level framework for determining appropriate AI use in assessments:

### Level 1: No AI
- **Description**: Assignment requires original thinking and authentic student work
- **Rationale**: Some assessments must measure students' independent cognitive abilities, original thought, and authentic demonstration of learning
- **Use Cases**: High-stakes assessments, authentic demonstrations of skill, assessments where the process is as important as the product
- **Considerations**: Ensures academic integrity and authentic assessment of student capabilities

### Level 2: AI Planning
- **Description**: AI can be used for planning, brainstorming, and initial structuring
- **Rationale**: AI assists with the planning phase but the core work remains student-generated
- **Use Cases**: Research planning, essay structuring, project organization
- **Considerations**: Students must demonstrate they can develop and execute their own ideas

### Level 3: AI Collaboration
- **Description**: AI can be used as a collaborative tool throughout the process
- **Rationale**: AI serves as a thinking partner, but students must critically engage with and refine AI-generated content
- **Use Cases**: Drafting with AI assistance, research support, idea development
- **Considerations**: Students must show evidence of critical evaluation and substantial contribution

### Level 4: Full AI
- **Description**: AI can be used extensively for content generation
- **Rationale**: Focus shifts to prompt engineering, critical evaluation, and synthesis of AI-generated content
- **Use Cases**: Content generation tasks where the learning objective is evaluation and synthesis
- **Considerations**: Assessment must measure students' ability to work with AI effectively and critically

### Level 5: AI Exploration
- **Description**: AI use is encouraged and expected, with focus on innovation and exploration
- **Rationale**: Students explore the boundaries of AI capabilities and develop innovative applications
- **Use Cases**: Advanced projects, creative applications, research into AI capabilities
- **Considerations**: Assessment focuses on innovation, critical thinking about AI, and ethical considerations

## Key Principles

1. **Transparency**: Students should clearly communicate their AI use
2. **Pedagogical Alignment**: AI use should align with learning objectives
3. **Critical Engagement**: Students must demonstrate critical thinking regardless of AI use level
4. **Authentic Assessment**: The assessment should still measure genuine learning
5. **Accessibility**: The framework should support diverse learners and needs

## Implementation Guidance

### For Instructors:
- Clearly communicate which AIAS level applies to each assessment
- Provide examples of acceptable AI use at each level
- Design assessments that align with the chosen AIAS level
- Ensure learning objectives are still met regardless of AI use
- Consider accessibility and equity in AI use policies

### For Students:
- Understand which AIAS level applies to each assignment
- Document AI use transparently
- Ensure AI use aligns with assignment requirements
- Demonstrate critical thinking and original contribution
- Use AI ethically and responsibly

## Notes on Updates (August 2024)

The August 2024 update reflects:
- Advances in generative AI technology (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, multimodal AI)
- Improved understanding of AI's impact on learning
- Feedback from global implementations
- Consideration of both acute and chronic problems in assessment
- Focus on pedagogical alignment rather than just academic integrity

The updated scale emphasizes the importance of aligning AI use with learning objectives and ensuring that assessments still measure genuine learning, regardless of the level of AI assistance allowed.
