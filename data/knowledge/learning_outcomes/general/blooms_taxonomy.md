# Bloom's Taxonomy - Learning Outcome Framework

## Overview
Bloom's Taxonomy provides a framework for categorizing educational learning objectives into levels of complexity and specificity.

## Six Levels (Revised)

### 1. Remember
Recall facts and basic concepts
- Define, list, name, recall, recognize

### 2. Understand
Explain ideas or concepts
- Classify, describe, discuss, explain, identify

### 3. Apply
Use information in new situations
- Execute, implement, solve, use, demonstrate

### 4. Analyze
Draw connections among ideas
- Differentiate, organize, relate, compare, contrast

### 5. Evaluate
Justify a stand or decision
- Appraise, argue, defend, judge, select, support

### 6. Create
Produce new or original work
- Design, assemble, construct, create, develop, formulate

## Writing Learning Outcomes

### Format
"Students will [action verb] [object] [context/condition]"

### Examples
- "Students will analyze primary source documents to understand historical context."
- "Students will create original research proposals following APA style guidelines."
- "Students will evaluate different theoretical frameworks for their applicability to real-world problems."

## Alignment Checklist

- [ ] Outcomes use measurable action verbs
- [ ] Outcomes align with course level (introductory, intermediate, advanced)
- [ ] Activities support outcome achievement
- [ ] Assessments measure outcome mastery
- [ ] Outcomes span multiple Bloom's levels (when appropriate)
